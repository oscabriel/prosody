## The shelf

The app opens on the shelf: every book in your library, with its cover.

Above it is **the nightstand**: the books you are reading now, the newest first, each with the device you last read it on. Opening a book puts it there. Finishing it, or setting it aside, takes it off. A book you set aside keeps your place, and opening it again puts it back.

Each book says how it stands: synced, syncing or not synced for a pair, and text alone, audio alone or printed pages for a book with one edition.

## The reading room

A book opens in its reading room.

### A synced book

- **Listen** starts the audio at your place. The sentence being read is marked, and the page follows it.
- **Tap a sentence** and the audio goes there.
- **Read without listening** and your place is the sentence you reached. Press Listen later, and the narrator picks up from it.
- **Speed** steps through 1×, 1.2×, 1.5×, 1.8×, 2× and 0.8×.
- **Contents** lists the chapters.

### Text alone

An EPUB with no audiobook reads the same way, without the player. Your place is still a sentence.

### Audio alone

An audiobook with no EPUB shows its cover, the chapter and a clock, with buttons to go back 15 seconds or on 30. Your place is a time.

### Printed pages

A PDF shows one page at a time, as tall as the window allows.

- Tap the right two thirds of the page, swipe left, or press the right arrow to turn on.
- Tap the left third, swipe right, or press the left arrow to turn back.
- Pinch to zoom in. A zoomed page moves under your finger and turns nothing.

Your place is a page.

## One place per book

Your place in a book is kept by Prosody, not by the device. Stop on your phone and the book opens at the same sentence on your laptop.

- In a synced book, reading and listening share one place, because every sentence has a time.
- A book you began as audio alone, and paired with its EPUB later, opens at the sentence being read at your time.
- If two devices disagree, the newer place wins.

## Finishing and setting aside

A book's reading room has "Finished" and "Set it aside". Either takes the book off the nightstand, and neither forgets your place. The shelf then says when you finished it or set it aside.
