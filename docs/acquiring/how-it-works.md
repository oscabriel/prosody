# How acquiring works

What the source does not say about the desk's fetching: why it is built as it is, and what breaks when it changes. Its words are in [`GLOSSARY.md`](GLOSSARY.md), and setting it up is [`README.md`](README.md).

## The desk

- The desk is `/desk`, a tool with the plain treatment: the same tokens, a sans face, no leaf. A book you want is a `Want` (Acquisition), keyed by the work it will be; the desk makes that work id (`w-` and twelve random hex digits) as you want it. A `WorkId` is `w-` and lower-case letters or digits, so the Api refuses any other a device sends, and a room's address with anything else is not found. `WantBook` starts the first search, `SearchAgain` another, `WithdrawWant` takes it off the desk. The `ReleaseSearch` policy asks the `Indexer` and answers `OfferReleases` (the ten most seeded audiobooks and the ten most seeded ebooks, each named as Prowlarr grabs it: `<indexer id>_<guid>`) or `RecordSearchFailed` with the reason; an answer to an older search, or to a book withdrawn, records nothing. A failed search waits for you to search again; nothing retries it. The `Wanted` view, keyed like the shelf, is what the desk watches, so offers appear as they come. No policy grabs anything.
- The Indexer is Prowlarr (`ProwlarrIndexer.ts`): one `GET /api/v1/search` with its API key, in Prowlarr's audiobook and ebook categories. Prosody holds Prowlarr's address and key only, as `INDEXER_URL` (no slash at the end) and `INDEXER_API_KEY` in `.env`; never a tracker login. Prowlarr down or refusing the key is a failed search the desk shows, not an error.
- Wanting a book catalogues its work: the `Cataloguing` policy asks `Metadata` once (`OpenLibraryMetadata.ts`: Open Library for the title, author, year and work; Audible's catalogue to find the audiobook's ASIN, since Audnexus has no search; Audnexus for its narrators, length and chapters) and sends `CatalogueWork` with no shelfmark and that description. Open Library has 8 seconds: a policy that dies within the platform's 10 second backstop is run again, one that dies later is not; the audiobook's lookups have 30 seconds and give no audiobook when they fail. Open Library may complete the names you gave (weir is Andy Weir), never replace them (Homer stays Homer). None of them needs an account, and they are sent a title and an author only. A wanted work is not on the shelf until it is shelved. A work the press found has no description, as every work catalogued before had.

## Grabs and shelving

- Grabbing is yours alone: no policy sends `GrabRelease`. It goes to the `Want`, which knows what the latest search offered, and refuses anything else (`ReleaseNotOffered`; a desk kept before grabs shows its old offers as "search again to grab it"). Each release a want grabs is a `Grab` (Acquisition), keyed `<work>.<n>`, which the `Grabbing` policy opens; the `Downloading` policy hands it to `Downloads`, and a download that can't be started is a stall with the reason. The press reports how each download goes: `RecordProgress`, `RecordStall`, `RecordPayloadArrived` and `RecordSeeding`, each recorded only when it is news. `ResumeGrab`, for any grab still downloading, starts its torrent again and asks the tracker for peers, or adds it again if qBittorrent does not have it (removed there by hand, or never added); progress also ends a stall. `AbandonGrab` only stops Prosody watching: the torrent and what came of it stay in qBittorrent, and the want keeps the release as grabbed, so it cannot be grabbed again.
- Downloads is qBittorrent (`QBittorrent.ts`, `QBittorrentDownloads.ts`), signed in afresh for each use with `DOWNLOADS_URL`, `DOWNLOADS_USERNAME` and `DOWNLOADS_PASSWORD` from `.env`. A grab's torrent file is fetched through Prowlarr by the release's `link` (Prowlarr's download link with its API key taken out, kept on every offer) and added under the category `DOWNLOADS_CATEGORY` names (`books` unless set), tagged with the grab's id: the tag is how the press finds a grab's torrent again. That category must save into the folder the press mounts as the downloads (`PROSODY_DOWNLOADS`): a payload is named by its path inside the category's save path. The press watches every minute (`Watch.ts`): a torrent still coming is stalled when nothing has come for half an hour, when qBittorrent stopped it, or when it reports an error or missing files; one that has all come names its payload, its file or folder inside the downloads.
- The seeding rule is `OBLIGATION` in `ReleasePayload.command.ts`: 72 hours within 30 days, one tracker's rule, and a constant of the model so that the model and the desk agree. `ReleasePayload` is refused with `StillObligated` and the hours owed until qBittorrent has seeded the payload that long (the desk shows the day they are owed by); then the `Releasing` policy removes it from qBittorrent's list with `deleteFiles=false`. Those hours are qBittorrent's `seeding_time`, which counts on while the tracker refuses its announces, so the desk asks you to check your tracker counts them before you release. Nothing in Prosody ever deletes or moves a payload.
- Shelving: on `PayloadArrived`, the `Shelving` policy names the work's folder from its catalogued title and author (`shelfmarkOf`, which keeps a name to one folder), claims it (`ShelfmarkClaim`, keyed by the shelfmark: the first work to claim a folder holds it, and every later claim is told whose it is with `ShelfmarkTaken`), and asks `Stacks`, the press (`/shelve`), to copy the edition in. A folder another work holds takes the edition for that work, so an audiobook grabbed for an ebook already on the shelf pairs with it. The press copies, never moves: it checks the library and the downloads answer within 8 seconds and the library is not empty, takes the one `.epub` or `.m4b` in the payload, copies it under a hidden `.part` name and renames it `<Title> - <Author>.<ext>`, so a walk never finds half a file, and answers `RecordShelved`, then walks the library at once. A library or downloads folder that does not answer, or a copy that fails, is `RecordNotShelved` with the reason, and the payload goes back in line five minutes later, so the ones behind it go on; a payload with no such file, or more than one, a folder that has that edition already, or a shelfmark that is not an author's folder and a title's, is `RecordNotShelved` for good. The press mounts the library writable, as its one writer, and the downloads read-only.

## Known limits

- qBittorrent can record an empty payload when a torrent's `content_path` is outside its save path.
- Wanting a book again loses its catalogue entry.
